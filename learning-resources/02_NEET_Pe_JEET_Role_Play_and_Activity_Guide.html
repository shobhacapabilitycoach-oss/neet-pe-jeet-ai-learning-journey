How to run the practice lab

  • Create triads: counsellor, student/parent and observer.

  • Allow 60 seconds to read the role card, 3–4 minutes to practise and 2 minutes for feedback.

  • Rotate roles so each learner practises at least twice.

  • On the second attempt, repeat only the moment that needs improvement.

  • Feedback formula: one observable strength, one specific adjustment and one sentence to try.

PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY: Use fictional learner situations. Feedback the behaviour you observed; do not label the learner as 'good' or 'bad' at sales.

Complete conversation model

Stage Observable behaviour Tool
1. Build Trust Create space; listen before advising. L.E.A.R.N.
2. Discover Follow clues and confirm the real need. C.L.U.E.
3. Parent Concern Understand what is underneath the first objection. H.E.A.R.
4. Recommend Connect need to relevant, accurate evidence. N.E.B.
5. Next Step Agree a clear, appropriate action without pressure. Clarity + choice

Student role cards

Persona 1 — The Stuck Performer

You study consistently and understand concepts, but your mock-test scores have plateaued. Under time pressure you rush and make avoidable mistakes.

OBSERVER FOCUS: Counsellor focus: trust, follow the clue, confirm before recommending.

Persona 2 — The Quiet Learner

You answer briefly and say only, 'I need better coaching.' You open up only when the counsellor asks calm, relevant follow-ups.

OBSERVER FOCUS: Counsellor focus: pacing, open questions, C.L.U.E.

Persona 3 — The Overloaded Student

School, tuition and self-study already fill your week. You want help but are worried about adding more hours.

OBSERVER FOCUS: Counsellor focus: understand workload before discussing a solution.

Persona 4 — The Content-Strong / Application-Weak Learner

You understand lessons but struggle to solve unfamiliar questions independently.

OBSERVER FOCUS: Counsellor focus: distinguish knowledge from application and recommend with relevance.

Parent role cards

Parent 1 — Investment Concern

You have already paid for coaching once and your child stopped following the schedule. Your real concern is repeating the same mistake.

OBSERVER FOCUS: Look for H.E.A.R.; the counsellor should uncover engagement/consistency before discussing price.

Parent 2 — Workload Protector

Your child already has school, tuition and self-study. You are worried another programme will add pressure.

OBSERVER FOCUS: Look for acknowledgement and exploration of the current routine.

Parent 3 — Evidence Seeker

You ask, 'How do I know this will actually help my child?' You want credible, relevant evidence rather than broad claims.

OBSERVER FOCUS: Look for clarification of what evidence would build confidence and avoidance of guarantees.

Parent 4 — Online-Learning Skeptic

You worry your child gets distracted online. Screen time is not the whole issue; engagement is.

OBSERVER FOCUS: Look for exploration before feature explanation.

Activities

Activity 1 — Follow the Clue

Give each pair a surface statement. The counsellor may ask only three questions. Every new question must connect to the learner's previous answer.

Activity 2 — Feature Diet

Give the counsellor six possible features and one diagnosed need. They may select only two and must explain why each matters.

Activity 3 — Concern Beneath the Words

Read a parent statement. Learners write two possible underlying concerns, then one neutral question to clarify rather than guess.

Activity 4 — Rewrite the Pitch

Convert a generic feature-led pitch into a Need → Evidence → Benefit recommendation.

Observer rubric

Criterion Behavioural evidence 1 2 3
Trust & listening Acknowledges the learner and creates space before advising. ☐ ☐ ☐
Discovery quality Asks relevant follow-ups; follows clues rather than a rigid script. ☐ ☐ ☐
Need confirmation Reflects the need back and checks understanding. ☐ ☐ ☐
Parent empathy Hears, explores and acknowledges before responding. ☐ ☐ ☐
Recommendation relevance Connects the identified need to accurate evidence and benefit. ☐ ☐ ☐
Boundaries & accuracy Avoids guarantees, pressure and unsupported claims. ☐ ☐ ☐
Next-step clarity Ends with a clear, appropriate next action. ☐ ☐ ☐

Rating guide: 1 = not yet demonstrated | 2 = partly demonstrated | 3 = consistently demonstrated

Feedback prompts

  • The strongest moment was… because…

  • The conversation became more/less open when…

  • One adjustment for the second attempt is…

  • A sentence you could try is…

  • What will you repeat in the field?
