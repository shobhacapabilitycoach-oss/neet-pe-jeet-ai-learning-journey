NEET Pe JEET | Core Facilitator Guide

A performance-first counselling workshop for learner-centred, consultative admission conversations

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Audience Sales Executives / Counsellors / Business Development Executives
Recommended duration 120 minutes
Modality Virtual instructor-led or classroom
Group size 12–25 learners
Business objective Improve counselling quality and conversion by helping executives diagnose learner needs, involve parents effectively and recommend with relevance.
Learning approach Scenario practice, reflection, peer learning, coaching feedback and workplace application

Learning objectives

  • Build trust in the opening moments of a student counselling conversation.

  • Use purposeful discovery questions to uncover the learner's real performance need.

  • Handle parent concerns with empathy, clarification and relevant evidence.

  • Connect identified needs to accurate solution evidence and meaningful learner benefits.

  • Integrate the complete conversation and agree an appropriate next step without pressure.

DESIGN PRINCIPLE: Diagnose before recommending. The programme shifts the counsellor from feature-led pitching to learner-centred decision support.

Recommended agenda

# Section Time Method Output
1 Opening & business context 10 min Data story + discussion Shared performance need
2 Build trust 20 min Demo + reflection L.E.A.R.N. behaviours
3 Discover before recommending 25 min Question practice + scenario C.L.U.E. discovery
4 Handle parent concerns 25 min H.E.A.R. practice Concern diagnosis
5 Recommend with relevance 20 min N.E.B. guided build Focused recommendation
6 Integrated simulation 15 min Role play + feedback Observed skill practice
7 Commitment & close 5 min Action plan 48-hour application

1. Opening and business context

PURPOSE: Create curiosity around conversion quality without blaming individuals.

Facilitator flow

  • Share anonymised funnel patterns: enquiries, meaningful conversations, follow-ups and conversions.

  • Ask learners where conversations most often lose momentum.

  • Frame the workshop around decision quality: understand the person, problem and decision-maker before recommending.

Suggested prompts

  • What makes a counselling conversation feel useful rather than sales-led?

  • Where do we tend to start explaining too early?

FACILITATOR WATCH-OUT: Use performance data to diagnose, not shame. Do not imply training alone causes conversion outcomes.

2. Build trust in the first conversation

PURPOSE: Help counsellors create psychological safety before advising.

Facilitator flow

  • Demonstrate a rushed opening versus a learner-centred opening.

  • Introduce L.E.A.R.N.: Listen, Explore, Acknowledge, Respond, Next Step.

  • Practise one opening with a hesitant student and one with a confident student.

Suggested prompts

  • What did the counsellor do before giving advice?

  • Which phrase created space for the learner to speak?

FACILITATOR WATCH-OUT: Trust is not friendliness alone; it is evidence that the counsellor is listening before solving.

3. Discover before you recommend

PURPOSE: Move from surface statements to a specific learner need.

Facilitator flow

  • Use common statements such as 'I need better coaching' or 'my scores are stuck'.

  • Introduce C.L.U.E.: Clarify, Listen, Uncover, Echo.

  • Practise follow-up questions that arise from the learner's previous answer.

  • Require a confirmation statement before any recommendation.

Suggested prompts

  • What clue did the learner just give you?

  • What do you still need to understand?

FACILITATOR WATCH-OUT: Avoid turning discovery into an interrogation. One relevant follow-up is better than a checklist of questions.

4. Handle parent concerns

PURPOSE: Treat parent concerns as information to understand, not objections to defeat.

Facilitator flow

  • Introduce H.E.A.R.: Hear, Explore, Acknowledge, Respond.

  • Practise concerns around investment, workload, online learning, evidence and previous disappointing experiences.

  • Ask learners to separate the first statement from the concern underneath it.

Suggested prompts

  • What is the parent protecting?

  • What would you need to know before responding?

FACILITATOR WATCH-OUT: Acknowledgement is not agreement. Avoid defensive claims, competitor criticism and unsupported promises.

5. Recommend with relevance

PURPOSE: Connect the diagnosed need to the smallest set of relevant, supportable solution evidence.

Facilitator flow

  • Introduce N.E.B.: Need, Evidence, Benefit.

  • Give learners feature cards and learner-need cards; ask them to select only one or two relevant features.

  • Rewrite generic product statements into learner-specific recommendations.

Suggested prompts

  • Why does this feature matter to this learner?

  • What can you leave out?

FACILITATOR WATCH-OUT: Do not promise score improvement or outcomes that cannot be guaranteed.

6. Integrated simulation

PURPOSE: Build fluency across the complete student-parent counselling journey.

Facilitator flow

  • Run triads: counsellor, student/parent, observer.

  • Use a five-decision scenario from enquiry through discovery, parent concern, recommendation and next step.

  • Observer gives one strength, one adjustment and one sentence to try.

  • Repeat the difficult section rather than replaying the entire conversation.

Suggested prompts

  • Where did the conversation change direction?

  • Which framework helped, and where did listening matter more than the framework?

FACILITATOR WATCH-OUT: Score observable behaviour, not personality or charisma.

7. Commitment and close

PURPOSE: Convert practice into a specific workplace behaviour.

Facilitator flow

  • Ask each learner to choose one behaviour to apply within 48 hours.

  • Use the commitment: 'In my next counselling conversation, instead of ___, I will ___.'.

  • Explain manager observation, peer practice and micro-nudge reinforcement.

Suggested prompts

  • What is the earliest real conversation where you can practise this?

  • What evidence will show the behaviour changed?

FACILITATOR WATCH-OUT: Avoid vague commitments such as 'communicate better'. Require one observable behaviour.
